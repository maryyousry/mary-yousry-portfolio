Background & Approach

Human-Centered Design Driven by Research

Bridging empathetic user research with structured design architecture to craft intuitive, accessible digital experiences.

I am a UI/UX Designer dedicated to crafting user-centered experiences through research-driven design. My goal is to understand not just what users do, but why they do it.

My design practice is anchored in rigorous user research, contextual interviews, competitive analysis, and intuitive information architecture. Whether I am sketching low-fidelity wireframes or building scalable Figma component libraries, I focus on identifying real user pain points, clarifying confusing pathways, and delivering interfaces that feel effortless to navigate.

I believe great design begins with active listening. By mapping user journeys and validating structures through low- and high-fidelity prototypes, I translate complex product requirements into clear, accessible, and delight-inducing digital experiences.

Having hands-on background in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React.js, Vite, Bootstrap, and Tailwind CSS allows me to design with technical feasibility in mind. I speak the developer's language, create well-structured design tokens, and facilitate smooth, error-free engineering handoffs without compromising UX fidelity.

Methodology

How I Solve Design Problems

A disciplined, research-driven UX process that balances user needs, business goals, and technical feasibility at every phase.

01

Empathize

Understand users, their context, emotional triggers, daily goals, and unarticulated pain points through generative interviews and qualitative observation.

Key Focus: User Interviews · Context Inquiries · Persona Empathy Maps
02

Define

Synthesize raw research data into actionable problem statements ("How Might We"), clear user personas, and prioritized product objectives.

Key Focus: Problem Statements · Affinity Mapping · User Journey Maps
03

Ideate

Explore divergent solution spaces, information architecture models, navigation schemes, and optimal user flows before committing to pixels.

Key Focus: Information Architecture · User Flows · Task Analysis
04

Wireframe

Create low-fidelity wireframes to validate structural hierarchy, content placement, and layout ergonomics quickly without visual distractions.

Key Focus: Low-Fidelity Layouts · Content Hierarchy · Structural Skeletons
05

Design

Craft high-fidelity interfaces backed by accessible color palettes, typography scales, responsive grids, and reusable Figma design systems.

Key Focus: High-Fidelity UI · Design System Variables · WCAG AA Contrast
06

Prototype

Build interactive micro-interactions and realistic clickable prototypes to test end-to-end task completion and navigation feedback.

Key Focus: Interactive Prototypes · Micro-interactions · Flow Validation
07

Refine

Conduct usability testing sessions, identify friction points, gather direct stakeholder feedback, and continuously iterate to polish the experience.

Key Focus: Usability Heuristic Audits · Friction Resolution · Design Polishing

UI/UX Design Case Study

Carto — E-Commerce Mobile Application

Role UI/UX Designer
Domain Mobile E-Commerce
Tools Figma · Miro · Notion
Deliverables Research, Wireframes, Hi-Fi, Tokens

The Problem

Mobile shoppers frequently abandon shopping carts due to confusing checkout flows, lack of clear product inventory indicators, and cluttered visual interfaces. In competitive e-commerce markets, cognitive friction during product discovery and multi-item checkout directly harms user retention and satisfaction.

Research & User Pain Points

User Friction 1: Multi-Step Checkout Ambiguity

Users expressed uncertainty regarding final shipping costs and payment methods until the very last screen, causing sudden hesitation and cart exits.

User Friction 2: Category Overload

Deep, nested navigation menus made quick browsing cumbersome on smaller mobile viewports.

Information Architecture & User Flow

The revised flow was streamlined into four frictionless milestones:

1. Home & Instant Search: Quick access to active deals, personalized recommendations, and clear category chips.
2. Product Detail Page (PDP): Immediate stock visibility, clear price hierarchy, user reviews, and prominent "Add to Bag" action.
3. Transparent Cart Drawer: Instant subtotal, delivery cost clarity, and quantity steppers without leaving context.
4. Express Checkout: Saved addresses, 1-tap payment methods, and an unambiguous order confirmation state.

Design System & UI Components

Established a modular component system built with strict Figma variables:

  • Primary Palette: Deep Blue (#30497D) for primary actions and brand anchoring; Burgundy (#A14646) for discount badges, urgent stock alerts, and accent CTAs.
  • Typography: Inter scale prioritizing high readability on small handheld screens.
  • Components: Modular product cards, sticky bottom checkout buttons, quantity controls, and consistent form error states.

Usability Takeaways

Testing demonstrated that consolidating checkout steps into a transparent summary view significantly reduced cognitive load and helped users complete orders with confidence.

UI/UX Design Case Study

Computer Science E-Learning Platform

Role UI/UX Designer
Domain EdTech / Computer Science
Tools Figma · Miro · User Interviews
Special Focus Pre-Course Diagnostic Tool

The Problem

Computer science students and career transitioners often struggle to choose the right courses. Existing online platforms list courses with vague prerequisites (e.g., "basic programming experience"), causing students to enroll in classes that are either too simplistic or overwhelming. This mismatch leads to high abandonment rates and loss of learner confidence.

User Research & Competitor Analysis

Competitor Analysis Insights

Major platforms (Coursera, Udemy, edX) organize courses by general tags and user star ratings, but do not provide an objective, interactive self-check mechanism prior to enrollment.

Student Pain Points

Interviews highlighted fear of wasting time on courses beyond their current capability, ambiguity around syllabus depth, and difficulty determining learning sequences.

The Star UX Feature: Pre-Course Assessment

Rather than relying on passive text descriptions, I designed an interactive Pre-Course Assessment. A short, non-intrusive diagnostic tool that invites learners to answer 3 targeted prerequisite questions. Based on their answers, the platform provides an immediate readiness recommendation: "Ready to Enroll", "Recommended Refresher Needed", or "Advanced Track Suggested".

Live Interactive UX Simulation Interactive Prototype Demo

Question: "What is your comfort level with JavaScript closures and asynchronous Promises?"

Information Architecture & Wireframes

Structured course pages around progressive disclosure: overview syllabus first, prerequisite self-check modal second, and curriculum breakdown with interactive timeline states.

Design System & Prototype Validation

The interface employs Dark Blue (#30497D) for academic trust and focus, paired with Burgundy (#A14646) accents for assessment progress indicators and personalized warnings. Feedback confirmed that the Pre-Course Assessment dramatically reduced student enrollment anxiety and provided immediate clarity.
